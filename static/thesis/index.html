The Social Media Radio Innovation and Its Impact on University Students' Ratings

An exploratory study proposing Epistemic Systems Theory and the LOKLOK platform, and testing, in a calibrated in-silico experiment anchored in a measured student wave, whether a social media radio can improve how students judge what they hear, without losing the audience.

The Question

Platform design, audience measurement, and epistemic quality are usually studied in separate literatures. This thesis joins them: can a radio platform be designed so that a university student who listens ends up judging what he or she hears more carefully, and can that design survive contact with the platform's own engagement pressure?

01Three layers of evidence, one honest hierarchy

Measured

The field wave

52 of 60 questionnaires from university students riding Uber in 6th of October City. Perceived benefit 5.12 (SD 0.87), correlated with the star rating (rho .67). It anchors the model; it is not a causal test.

See the measured results →
Qualitative

The assumption audit

193 segments coded by three independent human coders on a 25-code codebook, then tested against local machine coding under a pre-set gate. The gate was not met, and the thesis says so plainly.

Read the honest null →
Model

The calibrated experiment

500 synthetic personas in 8 design cells over 18 listening sessions, scored by a GRM-IRT model. The epistemic-first design raises the evaluative rating by +0.827 while engagement falls by 0.183.

Explore the trade-off →

02The headline result

Inside the model, the full LOKLOK design moves both audience ratings in the epistemic direction, and it does so at a measurable engagement cost. Intervals describe variation across generated personas, not human sampling precision.

The central finding is a trade-off: the epistemic gain is not free. Diversity and cooling (B) carry the contrast (+0.6265); the reputation path (A) adds +0.1905; moderation (C) matters mainly through its interaction with diversity (+0.3791).

03Assignment-robust, or not

A randomized-assignment triangulation re-ran the same eight cells under four designs. The diversity component and the engagement cost survive all of them; the BRS component does not: it collapses from +0.190 within personas to +0.038 between agents. That is the single most important methodological finding of the thesis.

04Epistemic Systems Theory, in four propositions

EST-P1

Architecture and credibility

The platform's structure is a condition of credibility, not a neutral container.

EST-P2

Diversity and cooling

Diversity-seeking selection and echo cooling constrain epistemic concentration.

EST-P3

Measurement

Measurement is an accountability requirement, reported separately from structural claims.

EST-P4

Participation

Participation and evidence-based production are the educational test, reserved for the field.

Verdicts: P1 partial and model-conditional; P2 supported within the model; P3 mixed (reliability yes, structural validity no); P4 untested, future work.
