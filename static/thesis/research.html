The research design, stated completely

This page presents the problem, the gap, the motivation, the aim, the objectives, the research questions, and the hypotheses exactly as the thesis defines them, with the status of each after the study. It is the formal counterpart of the thesis's Chapter 1.

1. The research problem

Platform design, audience measurement, and epistemic quality are studied in separate literatures. Social media radio combines algorithmic recommendation, live voice, and audience metrics, yet no framework links its architecture to a measurable audience rating of how well students judge what they hear. Meanwhile engagement pressure rewards the opposite of epistemic quality. The problem is not that platforms are bad; it is that their epistemic consequences are unspecified, unmeasured, and therefore unmanaged.

In formal terms: the thesis treats architecture as a treatment whose epistemic effect has no identified estimator in the literature, and provides one: a calibrated in-silico experiment anchored in a measured wave, with pre-declared state-outcome weights and a randomized-assignment triangulation.

2. The gap this research addresses

Gap 1

Disconnected literatures

Platform design, audience evaluation, and epistemic quality are studied separately; no construct links architecture to judgment.

Gap 2

No measurable epistemic rating

Filter bubbles and misinformation are described, but rarely connected to a measurable audience rating or a testable design.

Gap 3

Engagement pressure unmodeled

The trade-off between epistemic gain and engagement cost is asserted but not quantified; this thesis measures it (+0.827 versus -0.183).

Gap 4

Assignment-dependence ignored

Effects are reported without asking whether they survive different assignment designs; the triangulation shows the BRS path does not.

3. Motivation

  • Practical. Egyptian university radio and social audio need a design language that survives engagement pressure; LOKLOK is that specification.
  • Theoretical. The field lacks a falsifiable framework linking platform architecture to audience judgment; Epistemic Systems Theory (EST-P1 to EST-P4) supplies it.
  • Methodological. When a live trial is impossible, a calibrated in-silico experiment with declared weights, triangulated across assignment designs, is a reproducible way to make the design implications explicit before any deployment.
  • Policy. Misinformation governance needs mechanisms with named parameters (reputation, diversity, moderation, cooling, attention) that regulators and platforms can audit, rather than opaque ranking boxes.
  • Personal. The candidate works in radio and television education; the thesis is the honest, falsifiable version of the platform his students will actually use.

4. Aim and objectives

Aim. To propose Epistemic Systems Theory and the LOKLOK platform, and to test, with a calibrated in-silico experiment anchored in a measured student wave, whether the epistemic-first design raises evaluative and competence-format ratings without destroying engagement.

7. What is claimed, and what is deliberately not

Claimed

Model implications

What the specified design implies under declared weights, triangulated across assignment designs and passed through placebo and negative controls.

Claimed

Descriptive anchors

The 52-student wave anchors the model level and persona spread; its associations are descriptive.

Not claimed

Human treatment effects

No field RCT, no causal claim about students, no claim that the BRS should rank anyone consequentially.

Not claimed

Human NMLS validation

The competence profile is simulated from the GRM; no human administration is claimed.
