The Student Ratings, fully explained

Three numbers carry the thesis: +0.827, +11.30, and -0.183. This page explains exactly what each one is, how it is produced, how it is measured, and how a reviewer should and should not read it.

1. What the Student Ratings are

The Student Rating is the thesis's proposed measurement surface: two distinct formats in which a student's exposure to a broadcast is summarized, plus the engagement state that always accompanies them. They are deliberately not the platform's broadcaster reputation score (BRS); the BRS is a feature, the Student Ratings are the outcomes.

2. SR eval: the evaluative rating (1-7)

What it is. A seven-point evaluative judgment of the broadcast, produced inside the model from the latent credibility trajectory. G0 is anchored to the measured perceived-benefit mean (5.119), so the scale is tied to the real questionnaire, not free-floating.

The headline. The epistemic-first configuration G1 raises it by \(\Delta\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{eval}} = +0.827\) points, t(499) = 33.93, Cohen's \(d_z\) = 1.52, 95% interval [0.779, 0.875]. The interval describes variation across the 500 generated personas, not uncertainty about humans.

What moves it. B (diversity and cooling) contributes +0.6265; A (BRS) +0.1905; B x C +0.3791. Under between-agent assignment the total falls to +0.630 [0.384, 0.851], which is the interference finding.

3. SR comp: the competence profile (0-100)

What it is. A competence-format rating built from the published 35-item New Media Literacy Scale (NMLS) across its four domains (functional and critical consumption and prosumption), scored with the Samejima graded response model and rescaled to a 0-100 display. The instrument is used as published; it is not redefined.

The headline. \(\Delta\mathrm{SR}_{\mathrm{comp}} = +11.30\) points, t(499) = 33.74, \(d_z\) = 1.51.

The caveat, stated plainly. The responses are simulated from the GRM given each persona's latent trait; no human completed the NMLS in this study. Reliability (alpha .948, omega .953, ICC .953) is a property of the generating model, not human validation.

4. Engagement: the cost, measured

What it is. The declared engagement mapping,

$$\mathrm{eng} = 0.74 + 0.35(e - .30) - 0.10(\mathrm{dbi} - 1) + 0.08(q - .5)$$

so engagement rises with echo and falls with chosen diversity: the very shape of the trade-off. The thesis reports both the session average (-0.183) and the terminal measure (-0.217), and the cost per one evaluative point is about 0.22 (0.26 terminal). The G0 value falls from 0.713 to 0.530 in G1.

5. How scoring works

  1. Generate responses. For each persona and battery item, responses are drawn from the graded response model given the item difficulties \(b_k\) and discrimination \(a\).
  2. Score the profile. SR comp is the sum of expected category scores over the 35 NMLS items, rescaled to 0-100.
  3. Shift to the anchor. A single calibration shift pins the G0 evaluative mean to the measured PB mean of 5.119; the same shift applies to both arms, so the delta is unaffected by it.
  4. Report both frameworks. Frequentist paired intervals and a Bayesian one-sample normal summary of the same differences are shown side by side and labeled as re-expressions, not independent evidence.

7. Common misreadings to avoid

  • "The platform improves student judgment by 0.83 points." No: the model implies a contrast under declared assumptions; the human claim is explicitly future work.
  • "The narrow interval proves robustness." No: it reflects deterministic simulation given the seed and persona count.
  • "Reliability high, so the scales are valid." No: the CFA fit was poor, and structural validity is not claimed.
  • "Engagement fell, so the design fails." No: the trade-off is the contribution; both sides are reported as the result to manage.
