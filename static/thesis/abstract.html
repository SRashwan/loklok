Abstract

This thesis asks whether the design of a social media radio platform can improve how university students judge what they hear, without losing the audience. It proposes LOKLOK, a platform with four features: a reputation score for broadcasters, recommendations constrained to keep viewpoints varied, moderation with prompts for reflection, and a built-in measurement surface. Epistemic Systems Theory links these features to audience judgment.

Three evidence layers

A live trial was not possible, so the evidence has three layers. The measured layer is a questionnaire from 52 students (60 collected, 8 removed) riding Uber in 6th of October City, Giza. Perceived benefit averaged 5.12 on a seven-point scale (SD = 0.87) and correlated with the star rating (ρ = .67). The credibility and perceived-benefit scales were reliable (α = .96 and .95), but the factor model fitted poorly, so no structural validity is claimed. The qualitative layer is a group-session transcript and a researcher's self-report. Three coders labelled 193 usable segments with good agreement (pairwise κ = .76 to .86), yet the transcripts contain little participant speech about the mechanisms. A local 7B model and an assistant first pass did not reach the pre-set reliability rule for any mechanism code, so this layer serves as an audit of assumptions, not as model input. The modelling layer is a calibrated in-silico experiment: 500 synthetic personas drawn from the distribution of the 52 respondents were run through eight combinations of three design components over 18 sessions, with the status-quo arm anchored to the measured mean.

The model result

In the model, the full design raised the evaluative rating by 0.83 points (interval 0.78 to 0.88 across generated personas) and a competence-format rating by 11.3 points on a 100-point scale, while engagement fell by 0.18 on a 0 to 1 scale. When treatment was assigned between agents on the network instead of within personas, the evaluative gain fell to 0.63 (0.38 to 0.85). The diversity component (+0.63) and the engagement cost held in every design, while the reputation component shrank from +0.19 to +0.04, so reputation is interference-sensitive. Across 256 draws that varied the assumed weights, the contrast was positive in 96.5% of cases but ranged from −0.24 to +1.81, and it depended mainly on one declared link between exposure diversity and credibility.

Boundaries

These are model-based implications from a convenience sample, not treatment effects; no field causal test is claimed. The thesis offers an explicit route from theory to a testable platform design and a plan for a real deployment.

Keywords: social media radio; Epistemic Systems Theory; misinformation governance; broadcaster reputation; agent-based modelling; calibrated in-silico experiment; psychometric reporting.
