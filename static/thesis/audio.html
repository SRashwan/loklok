The audio and signal lab

LOKLOK is a radio platform: everything it measures passes through audio. This page presents what the measured-wave riders assessed, the signal processing a working platform needs (FFT, spectrograms, and audio transformers), and the Dirichlet programming model that decides what plays each hour.

1. What the Uber riders listened to

The measured wave asked 52 university students about a social media radio listening episode during an Uber ride, with the post-broadcast battery completed at the end of the episode. The archive contains the questionnaire, the 25-topic Arabic topic list, and the researcher's 27.7-minute self-report recording; the original broadcast recordings the riders heard are not in the archive, and the thesis says so. The samples below are therefore synthesized illustrations of the episode kinds the questionnaire describes (bulletin, talk, music, correction, promo), generated for this lab. The self-report audio itself is preserved in the raw package.

3. FFT and spectrum analysis

The Fourier transform is how a radio platform hears. The discrete transform and its efficient radix-2 form:

$$X_k = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_n\, e^{-2\pi i\, kn/N}, \qquad X_k = \sum_{\text{even}} x_{2m} e^{-2\pi i km/(N/2)} + e^{-2\pi i k/N}\sum_{\text{odd}} x_{2m+1} e^{-2\pi i km/(N/2)}$$

Windowing tames spectral leakage (a Hann window \(w_n = 0.5\,(1 - \cos(2\pi n/(N-1)))\)), and a spectrogram is a stack of short-window FFTs along time. In the thesis's pipeline, the analogue is the attention gate: the EMA probe samples the listener's state at a moment, like a short-time window samples the signal.

Click a block for its role. The transformer output is an audio embedding that joins the text embedding block: in the thesis's current run the audio path is proposed, not executed, and it is labeled as such.

6. Dirichlet radio programming

The programming question: what mix of categories should each hour contain? The Dirichlet distribution is the natural answer: a probability distribution over probability vectors.

$$x \sim \mathrm{Dir}(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_K), \qquad p(x) \propto \prod_{k=1}^{K} x_k^{\,\alpha_k - 1}, \qquad H = -\sum_k x_k \log x_k$$

Reading the parameters. Alpha below 1 concentrates mass on a few categories (specialized hours); alpha above 1 spreads it (balanced hours). Drawing one vector per hour gives the day's programming targets; MORE then ranks the best item inside each drawn mix. This is how the platform's schedule inherits the diversity constraint: the constraint lives in the prior, not in a hand-tuned timetable.

Draw your own broadcast day

Each hour is one Dirichlet draw over the categories; the bar colors show the winning category, and the entropy H measures how balanced the day is. Try all alphas at 0.3 (specialized hours), then all at 3 (balanced programming).
